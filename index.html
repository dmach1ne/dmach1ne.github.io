SungJae (Daniel) Ahn

I am an undergraduate student in Computer Science and Engineering at Kyung Hee University, supervised by Prof. Hyoseok Hwang at AIR Lab.

My conception of a fully realized AI is an agent that continually learns and adapts as it accumulates experience in a world bigger than itself. To do so, it must build on that experience to quickly identify effective actions and make informed decisions about how to modify its own behavior or structure. This goal motivates my interest in Continual Reinforcement Learning, Exploration, and Meta-learning for RL.

Currently, I am interested in replacing exploration strategies based on human experience with strategies that agents learn from their own experience.

Publications
Re-exploration by Leveraging Forgetting in Random Network Distillation
SungJae Ahn, Hyoseok Hwang
Submitted to Korea Software Congress 2026
paper

How should agents explore in changing environments, where past experience may no longer be valid? While it is reasonable to revisit stale experiences, explicitly tracking the recency of each experience is impractical. In this work, we guide and leverage forgetting in the RND predictor network, thereby injecting optimism into old experiences and enabling rapid adaptation to changing environments.
Revisiting Temporal Regularization for Smooth Control in Deep Reinforcement Learning
SungJae Ahn, Jeong Woon Lee, Kyoleen Kwak, Hyoseok Hwang
Submitted to ICRA 2027
project page / paper / alphaXiv / code

In this work, we show that the temporal penalty, previously considered unrelated to spatial smoothness, can in fact induce this very property through shared next states, improving robustness to observation noise. Our extensive experiments show that, by avoiding direct spatial regularization, CATS can achieve both forms of smoothness while better preserving task performance.

Analyzing Reasoning Diversity in Self-Distillation
Advanced Deep Learning, Spring 2026
presentation

SDPO uses a single successful rollout to supervise others, explicitly reinforcing a narrow set of reasoning strategies. We demonstrated that utilizing only text feedback from failed rollouts can encourage diverse strategies. We also found that, together with SDPO's top-K approximation, the direction of KL divergence has different effects depending on the outcome. For example, when the student fails, the reverse KL used by SDPO provides a weaker learning signal than forward KL for selecting new words because the student already assigns low probability to promising tokens.
